## Summary

Standards Track (2009, replacing experimental RFC 4138). A
retransmission timeout can fire when nothing was lost, only delayed.
F-RTO is a sender-only way to notice that after the fact and avoid
resending a whole window for nothing. Also sums up the two other
detection methods, Eifel (timestamps) and DSACK.

## Key claims

- TCP has two ways to trigger a retransmission: duplicate ACKs and the timer. "The Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) [Pos81] has two methods for triggering retransmissions." (1)
- On an RTO the congestion window goes to one segment and slow start resends. "the congestion window is initialized to one segment and unacknowledged segments are retransmitted using the slow-start algorithm." (1)
- The timer can fire with nothing lost. "the retransmission timer can expire spuriously and cause unnecessary retransmissions when no segments have been lost" (1)
- After a spurious timeout the late ACKs cause a whole window to be resent. "the late acknowledgments of the original segments arrive at the sender, usually triggering unnecessary retransmissions of a whole window of segments during the RTO recovery." (1)
- Causes are delay spikes: mobile handoffs, a switch to a slower path, competing traffic on a slow link, link-layer retries. "some mobile networking technologies involve sudden delay spikes on transmission because of actions taken during a hand-off." (1)
- F-RTO works on the sender alone and needs no TCP options. "F-RTO is a TCP sender-only algorithm that does not require any TCP options to operate." (Abstract)
- How it decides: after the RTO retransmit, send new data; if the next two ACKs advance the window, the timeout was spurious. "If the second acknowledgment that arrives after the timeout advances the window (i.e., acknowledges data that was not retransmitted), the F-RTO sender declares the timeout spurious and exits the RTO recovery." (1)
- Eifel uses timestamps; DSACK lets the receiver report duplicates. "The Eifel algorithm uses TCP timestamps [BBJ92] for detecting a spurious timeout upon arrival of the first acknowledgment after the retransmission." and "With DSACK, the TCP receiver can report if it has received a duplicate segment, enabling the sender to detect afterwards whether it has retransmitted segments unnecessarily." (1)
- Eifel and DSACK also catch unnecessary retransmits caused by reordering; F-RTO only handles timeouts. "Eifel and DSACK can also be used for detecting unnecessary retransmissions caused by other events, such as packet reordering." (1)
- Without a specific response algorithm, respond to a spurious timeout conservatively. "If no specific response algorithm is implemented, the TCP SHOULD respond to spurious timeout conservatively, applying the TCP congestion control specification [APB09]." (4)
