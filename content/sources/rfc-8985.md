## Summary

Standards-track RFC from February 2021, written by the Google engineers
who built it in Linux. Explains why counting three duplicate ACKs misses
losses at the end of a burst, lost retransmissions and reordering, and
replaces it with time-based loss detection (RACK) plus a probe that
turns tail timeouts into fast recovery (TLP).

## Key claims

- RACK-TLP is recommended as the alternative to duplicate-ACK counting. "it is RECOMMENDED as an alternative to that earlier approach." (1)
- Classic fast recovery starts after 3 duplicate ACKs. "a sender starts fast recovery when the number of DupAcks received reaches a threshold (DupThresh) that defaults to 3" (1.1)
- Fast recovery only halves the congestion window. "The sender also halves the congestion window during the recovery." (1.1)
- The RTO is SRTT plus four times the variation, at least 1 second. "The RTO timer interval is conservatively the smoothed RTT (SRTT) plus four times the RTT variation, and is lower bounded to 1 second [RFC6298]." (1.1)
- An RTO resets the congestion window to one segment. "resets the congestion window to the loss window value (by default, 1 full-sized segment [RFC5681])" (1.1)
- Fast recovery is preferred; the RTO should be a last resort. "Fast recovery is the preferred form of loss recovery because it can potentially recover all losses in the timescale of a single round trip, with only a fractional congestion window reduction." (1.2)
- Losing the last segment of a flight produces no duplicate ACKs, so only the RTO can fix it. "Consider an example where only the last segment in a flight of 100 segments is lost. Lacking any DupAck, the sender RTO expires, reduces the congestion window to 1" (1.2)
- Lost retransmissions also fall back to RTO. "Lost retransmissions cause a resort to RTO recovery since DupAck counting does not detect the loss of the retransmissions." (1.2)
- Reordering past the threshold causes spurious recovery. "If the reordering degree is beyond DupThresh, DupAck counting can cause a spurious fast recovery and unnecessary congestion window reduction." (1.2)
- Reordering happens in real networks: Wi-Fi, link bonding, ECMP. "Link-layer protocols (e.g., 802.11 block ACK), link bonding, or routers' internal load balancing (e.g., ECMP) can deliver TCP segments out of order." (1.2)
- With SACK, the receiver reports segments it holds beyond a gap, which is what lets RACK mark the earlier ones lost. "Receive P2, SACK P2 & P3" (3.4, Figure 1, event 6); "The delivery of P3 enables RACK to infer (Event 5b) that P1 and P2 were likely lost because they were sent before P3." (3.4)
- Request/response traffic benefits most. "RACK-TLP is particularly useful when the sender is limited by the application, which can happen with interactive or request/response traffic." (9.1)
- TLP probes with new data or the last segment. "TLP sends the next available (new) segment or retransmits the last (highest-sequenced) segment in two round trips to probe the network" (3.2)
- RACK's idea: use send times and SACK to infer loss. "RACK's key innovation is using per-segment transmission timestamps and widely deployed SACK [RFC2018] options to conduct time-based inferences instead of inferring losses by counting ACKs or SACKed sequences." (3.1)
- A segment is lost if something sent later got through and it hasn't arrived after an RTT plus a reordering window. (3.3.1, the two conditions)
- The reordering window is bounded, and the bound should be SRTT. "The RACK reordering window MUST be bounded, and this bound SHOULD be SRTT." (3.3.2)
- TLP sends one probe to get an ACK instead of waiting for the RTO. "Tail Loss Probe (TLP) triggers ACK feedback by quickly sending a probe segment to avoid retransmission timeout (RTO) events." (1)
- The 100-segment example: last three lost costs three RTTs plus an RTO without TLP; with TLP, four RTTs and only a partial window cut. "The total recovery time is four RTTs, and the congestion window is only partially reduced instead of being fully reset." (3.2)
- TLP fires about two round trips after the last send. (3.2; Figure 1 event 3a, "2RTTs after (2), TLP timer fires")
- On RTO, RACK only marks the first segment lost, reducing spurious retransmissions. (3.5)

Added 2026-09-29 for `tcp-retransmission` (deep, re-opened):

- The reordering window starts at zero or small, grows with DSACK, and is capped at SRTT. "The RACK reordering window SHOULD adaptively increase ... if the sender receives a Duplicate Selective Acknowledgment (DSACK) option [RFC2883]." (3.3.2, rule 2)
- Short flows accept spurious retransmits to recover faster. "For short flows, the low initial reordering window helps recover losses quickly, at the risk of spurious retransmissions." (3.3.2)
- Starting window min_RTT/4, from Linux's experience; after N DSACK round trips it's (N+1) * min_RTT/4, reset after 16 recoveries. "it starts with a conservative RACK.reo_wnd of RACK.min_RTT/4." and "RACK persists using the inflated RACK.reo_wnd for up to 16 loss recoveries" (6.2, Step 4)
- Every transmission, original or retransmission, can reveal losses sent before it. "every data segment, whether it is an original data transmission or a retransmission, can be used to detect losses of the segments sent chronologically prior to it." (9.1)
- Lost retransmissions happen under token-bucket policers. "such a lost retransmission can happen when TCP is being rate-limited, particularly by token bucket policers with a large bucket depth and low rate limit" (9.1, example 2)
- Drawback: per-segment send times, which the older SACK scoreboard approach doesn't need, useful in data centers where RTT is below clock granularity. "this is particularly useful on ultra-low RTT networks where the RTT may be less than the sender TCP clock granularity (e.g., inside data centers)." (9.1)
- The reordering timer can fire early and cause spurious retransmits, more so without DSACK. "the reordering timer may expire prematurely (like any other retransmission timer) and cause higher spurious retransmissions, especially if DSACK is not supported." (9.1)
- RACK-TLP doesn't change the RTO computation or detect spurious RTOs; it works with F-RTO and Eifel. "It neither changes the RTO timer calculation nor detects spurious RTOs." (9.2)
