## Summary

A 2023 best current practice (BCP 237) that gathers the DNSSEC RFCs in
one place and states that DNSSEC is the best current practice for
authenticating DNS data. It also records how little DNSSEC was deployed
when it was written, the history of the spec, and which algorithms are
in use.

## Key claims

- DNSSEC is the only standards-track way to authenticate DNS data. "To date, no Standards Track RFCs offer any other method for such origin authentication of data in the DNS." (1.1)
- Deployment is low: when written, fewer than 10% of website domain names were signed. "Recent estimates are that fewer than 10% of the domain names used for websites are signed, and only around a third of queries to recursive resolvers are validated." (1.1)
- Low deployment reflects cost vs value. "it just indicates that the value of deploying DNSSEC is often considered lower than the cost." (1.1)
- The TLDs in the root are almost all signed. "the near-universal deployment of DNSSEC for the TLDs in the DNS root zone" (1.1)
- Current DNSSEC is the third try. "What we refer to as \"DNSSEC\" is the third iteration of the DNSSEC specification; [RFC2065] was the first, and [RFC2535] was the second." (2)
- The root was signed in 2010. "particularly after the DNS root was signed in 2010." (2.1)
- RSA is still among the most popular signing algorithms. "RSA is still among the most popular signing algorithms for DNSSEC." (2)
- ECDSA and EdDSA have become popular. "ECDSA and EdDSA have become very popular signing algorithms in recent years." (3)
- The root trust anchor was rolled in 2018 with RFC 5011. "This method was used in 2018 to update the DNS root trust anchor." (4)
- DS records hold a digest of the child's key; IANA keeps a registry of the digest algorithms. "DNSSEC DS RRtype digest algorithms" (6)
- NSEC3 is now as much core DNSSEC as NSEC. "It makes NSEC3 [RFC5155] as much a part of DNSSEC as NSEC is." (2.1)
- Minimal NSEC records signed on demand stop zone walking. "authoritative name servers can effectively stop the disclosure of zone contents otherwise made possible by walking the chain of NSEC records in a signed zone" (5, quoting RFC 4470)
- CDS and CDNSKEY records help automate DS maintenance in the parent. "[RFC7344] describes using the CDS and CDNSKEY resource records to help automate the maintenance of DS records in the parents of signed zones." (4)
