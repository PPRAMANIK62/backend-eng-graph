## Summary

A 2022 standard, written by people from three CDNs, for cache-control
headers aimed at one class of cache. Defines `CDN-Cache-Control`, which
CDNs obey and browsers ignore.

## Key claims

- Real deployments stack several cache layers. "Modern deployments of HTTP often use multiple layers of caching." (1)
- Example layers: origin cache, same-network cache, CDNs, browser. "it might use one or more CDNs that are distributed throughout the Internet, and it might benefit from browser caching as well." (1)
- A publisher that can purge its CDN may give it a longer lifetime than other caches. "if a publisher has a mechanism to invalidate the contents of a cache that it has a relationship with (such as a CDN cache), they might be more comfortable assigning a more generous caching policy to it while still wanting to restrict the behavior of other caches." (1)
- Cache-Control can't target most directives at one class of cache. "However, it is relatively undifferentiated" (1)
- The CDN header, with an example. "CDN-Cache-Control: max-age=60" (2)
- The authors are from Akamai, Fastly and Cloudflare (S. Ludin, Akamai; M. Nottingham, Fastly; Y. Wu, Cloudflare). (header)
- CDN-Cache-Control is aimed at CDN caches only. "the CDN-Cache-Control response header field, which is targeted at content delivery network (CDN) caches." (Abstract)
