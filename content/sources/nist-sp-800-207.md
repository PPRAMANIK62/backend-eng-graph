## Summary

NIST's definition of zero trust (2020): no trust from network location
or ownership, per-request (per-session) authentication and authorization
decided by a policy engine and enforced at a policy enforcement point
close to the resource. Lists seven tenets, the logical components,
deployment models, threats to a zero trust design, and migration.

## Key claims

- Zero trust is a set of ideas, not one product. "Zero trust (ZT) is the term for an evolving set of cybersecurity paradigms that move defenses from static, network-based perimeters to focus on users, assets, and resources." (Abstract)
- Migration is incremental, not a wholesale replacement. "Implementing a ZTA is a journey rather than a wholesale replacement of infrastructure or processes." (Section 7)
- No trust from location or ownership. "Zero trust assumes there is no implicit trust granted to assets or user accounts based solely on their physical or network location (i.e., local area networks versus the internet) or based on asset ownership (enterprise or personally owned)." (Abstract)
- Authn and authz happen before a session to a resource. "Authentication and authorization (both subject and device) are discrete functions performed before a session to an enterprise resource is established." (Abstract)
- Protect resources, not network segments. "Zero trust focuses on protecting resources (assets, services, workflows, network accounts, etc.), not network segments" (Abstract)
- Operative definition: least-privilege per-request decisions, network viewed as compromised. "least privilege per-request access decisions in information systems and services in the face of a network viewed as compromised." (Section 2)
- Perimeter firewalls don't help with attacks from inside. "less useful for detecting and blocking attacks from inside the network" (Section 2)
- Access goes through a policy decision point (PDP) and policy enforcement point (PEP). (Section 2, Figure 1)
- The implicit trust zone behind a PEP should be as small as possible; zero trust moves the PDP/PEP closer to the resource. "To allow the PDP/PEP to be as specific as possible, the implicit trust zone must be as small as possible." (Section 2, airport example)
- Tenet 2: all communication secured regardless of location. "Network location alone does not imply trust." (2.1)
- Tenet 3: access per session, least privilege; access to one resource doesn't grant another. "However, authentication and authorization to one resource will not automatically grant access to a different resource." (2.1)
- Tenet 4: dynamic policy using identity (user or service), device state, behaviour and environment. (2.1)
- Client identity can be a service identity. "client identity can include the user account (or service identity)" (2.1, tenet 4)
- Network assumption: act as if an attacker is on the network. "Assets should always act as if an attacker is present on the enterprise network" (2.2)
- Components: policy engine (decides), policy administrator (sets up or tears down the path), policy enforcement point (enables, monitors, ends connections). (Section 3)
- The PE/PA are critical and a target; misconfiguration or compromise opens access. (5.1)
- If the PEP or PE/PA are unreachable, operations stop. "An operational error could prevent an entire enterprise from functioning if the policy engine or policy administrator component becomes inaccessible from the network." (5.2)
- Stolen credentials still work within their granted access; ZT limits lateral movement. "However, an attacker with valid credentials (or a malicious insider) may still be able to access resources for which the account has been granted access." (5.3)
- Migration is gradual; most will run a hybrid for a long time. "Most enterprise infrastructures will operate in a hybrid zero trust/perimeter-based mode" (Executive summary area)
- The tenets apply inside an organization, not to anonymous public users. (2.1, after the tenets)
- Why: remote users, BYOD, cloud. "Zero trust is a response to enterprise network trends that include remote users, bring your own device (BYOD), and cloud-based assets that are not located within an enterprise-owned network boundary." (Abstract)
- Policy changes must be logged and audited. "Mitigating associated risks means the PE and PA components must be properly configured and monitored, and any configuration changes must be logged and subject to audit." (5.1)
- Migrate one business process at a time. "Migration to a ZTA approach to the enterprise may take place one business process at a time." (7.2)
