## Summary

Best Current Practice 142: how a NAT should treat TCP. The part a
backend engineer meets most is the idle timeout: how long a NAT must keep
state for a quiet connection before it may forget it.

## Key claims

- A NAT must reuse the same public mapping for one internal address and port, whoever it talks to. "REQ-1: A NAT MUST have an "Endpoint-Independent Mapping" behavior for TCP." (4.1, REQ-1)
- An established TCP connection can sit idle forever; default keepalives are every 2 hours. "by default, such keep-alive packets are sent every 2 hours if enabled [RFC1122]." (5. NAT Session Refresh)
- Minimum idle timeouts. "the value of the "established connection idle-timeout" MUST NOT be less than 2 hours 4 minutes. The value of the "transitory connection idle-timeout" MUST NOT be less than 4 minutes." (5. NAT Session Refresh, REQ-5)
- Some NATs reap idle sessions proactively. "other NATs may choose to proactively reap idle sessions." (5. NAT Session Refresh, REQ-5 justification)
- Unsolicited inbound SYNs: wait at least 6 seconds, then usually reply with ICMP port unreachable. "A NAT MUST NOT respond to an unsolicited inbound SYN packet for at least 6 seconds after the packet is received." (4.3, REQ-4)
- A NAT must support hairpinning for TCP. "REQ-8: A NAT MUST support "hairpinning" for TCP." (7.2)

## My notes

- The spec gives minimums. Real NATs, firewalls and cloud load balancers
  often time out much sooner; no opened source gives their numbers, so
  articles should say "check yours" rather than quote one.
