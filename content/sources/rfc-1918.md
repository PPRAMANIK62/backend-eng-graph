## Summary

BCP 5. Reserves three IPv4 blocks for private networks that anyone can
use without asking a registry, on the condition that they never appear
on the public Internet. Updated by RFC 6761 (rfc-editor.org info page).

## Key claims

- The three private blocks are 10/8, 172.16/12 and 192.168/16. "10.0.0.0        -   10.255.255.255  (10/8 prefix)" (3)
- Anyone can use them without asking. "An enterprise that decides to use IP addresses out of the address space defined in this document can do so without any coordination with IANA or an Internet registry." (3)
- They're unique only inside the enterprise. "Addresses within this private address space will only be unique within the enterprise" (3)
- Private hosts can't have direct IP connectivity outside; they reach out through gateways. "they cannot have IP connectivity to any host outside of the enterprise." (3)
- Routes for private blocks must not leak, and ISPs should filter them. "routing information about private networks shall not be propagated on inter-enterprise links" (3)
- Moving a host between private and public means renumbering. "Moving a host from private to public or vice versa involves a change of IP address" (3)
