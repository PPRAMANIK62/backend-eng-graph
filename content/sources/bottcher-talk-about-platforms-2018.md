## Summary

Essay from a Thoughtworks consultant defining a digital platform as a
self-service internal product that delivery teams build on, and arguing
that its job is to cut "backlog coupling" (teams waiting on other teams'
tickets). Widely used as the definition behind platform engineering.

## Key claims

- The definition. "A digital platform is a foundation of self-service APIs, tools, services, knowledge and support which are arranged as a compelling internal product." (What is a 'Platform' anyway?)
- The point is faster delivery with less coordination. "Autonomous delivery teams can make use of the platform to deliver product features at a higher pace, with reduced co-ordination." (same)
- Backlog coupling: a team's work needs matching work items in another team's backlog. "if a large number of items in the backlog of a digital product team require a corresponding backlog item to be raised in another team" (The impact of "backlog coupling")
- A good platform is self-service, with no tickets. "The platform must provide services that do not require tickets to be raised and work to be assigned." (same)
- Platforms have to be worth using, not mandated. "platforms must be compelling to use, they cannot stand on a mandate alone." (Platform as an internal product)
