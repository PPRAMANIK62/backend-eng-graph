## Summary

A short statement by fifteen threat modeling practitioners: what threat
modeling is, the four questions it answers, the values and principles
behind doing it well, and patterns and anti-patterns. It's
methodology-agnostic on purpose.

## Key claims

- Definition. "Threat modeling is analyzing representations of a system to highlight concerns about security and privacy characteristics." (What is threat modeling?)
- The four questions: what are we working on, what can go wrong, what are we going to do about it, did we do a good enough job. "At the highest levels, when we threat model, we ask four key questions:" (What is threat modeling?)
- Anyone can and should do it. "You. Everyone. Anyone who is concerned about the privacy, safety, and security of their system." (Who should threat model?)
- It's a guide, not a method. "The Manifesto contains ideas, but is not a how-to, and is methodology-agnostic." (How should I use the Threat Modeling Manifesto?)
- Values include continuous refinement. "Continuous refinement over a single delivery." (Values)
- Values: design issues over compliance. "A culture of finding and fixing design issues over checkbox compliance." (Values)
- Early and frequent analysis. "The best use of threat modeling is to improve the security and privacy of a system through early and frequent analysis." (Principles)
- Follow design changes in small iterations. "Threat modeling must align with an organization’s development practices and follow design changes in iterations that are each scoped to manageable portions of the system." (Principles)
- Anti-pattern: the hero. "Threat modeling does not depend on one’s innate ability or unique mindset; everyone can and should do it." (Hero Threat Modeler)
- Anti-pattern: only analysing. "Go beyond just analyzing the problem; reach for practical and relevant solutions." (Admiration for the Problem)
- Anti-pattern: one perfect diagram. "It is better to create multiple threat modeling representations because there is no single ideal view, and additional representations may illuminate different problems." (Perfect Representation)
- Anti-pattern: overfocus. "Avoid exaggerating attention on adversaries, assets, or techniques." (Tendency to Overfocus)
