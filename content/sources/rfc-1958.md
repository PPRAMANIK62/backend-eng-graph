## Summary

A short IAB document listing the Internet's architectural principles as
of 1996: the end-to-end argument, fate-sharing, and why datagrams won
over virtual circuits. Updated by RFC 3439.

## Key claims

- End-to-end functions belong in end-to-end protocols. "It is also generally felt that end-to-end functions can best be realised by end-to-end protocols." (2.3)
- The network will fail at some rate, so the ends own integrity. "The best way to cope with this is to accept it, and give responsibility for the integrity of communication to the end systems." (2.3)
- State belongs only at the endpoints (fate-sharing), so datagrams beat virtual circuits. "An immediate consequence of this is that datagrams are better than classical virtual circuits." (2.3)
