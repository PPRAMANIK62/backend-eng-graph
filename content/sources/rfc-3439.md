## Summary

An informational RFC on simplicity in network design. Section 3,
"Layering Considered Harmful", argues that strict layering hides
information that other layers need and adds complexity.

## Key claims

- Strict layering forces each layer to finish before the next, so each is optimized alone. "This means that the optimization of each layer has to be done separately." (3)
- Multiplexing and segmentation hide information lower layers need. "the operations of multiplexing and segmentation both hide vital information that lower layers may need to optimize their performance." (3)
- Layers duplicate work and need each other's data. "layer N may need layer N-2 information (e.g., lower layer packet sizes)" (3)
- More layers tends to mean more complexity and cost. "Industry experience has taught us that increased layering frequently increases complexity and hence leads to increases in OPEX" (3)
- Conclusion: prefer horizontal separation. "horizontal (as opposed to vertical) separation may be more cost-effective and reliable in the long term." (3)
