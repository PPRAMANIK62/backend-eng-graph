## Summary

The HTTP/3 spec: HTTP semantics mapped onto QUIC. Its introduction
explains why HTTP/2 over TCP still stalls on loss, which is what
motivated moving to QUIC.

## Key claims

- HTTP/1.1 opens many TCP connections for parallelism, which hurts congestion control. "since TCP does not share congestion control across multiple connections." (1.1)
- HTTP/2 multiplexes over one TCP connection, and one lost packet stalls every request. "because the parallel nature of HTTP/2's multiplexing is not visible to TCP's loss recovery mechanisms, a lost or reordered packet causes all active transactions to experience a stall regardless of whether that transaction was directly impacted by the lost packet." (1.1)
- QUIC gives per-stream reliability and connection-wide congestion control. "By providing reliability at the stream level and congestion control across the entire connection, QUIC has the capability to improve the performance of HTTP compared to a TCP mapping." (1.2)
- QPACK lets the encoder limit how much head-of-line blocking header compression causes. "[QPACK] describes a variation of HPACK that gives an encoder some control over how much head-of-line blocking can be caused by compression." (4.2.1)
