## Summary

How QUIC uses TLS 1.3 (2021). QUIC carries the TLS handshake messages
itself and uses the keys TLS produces to encrypt its own packets; there
are no TLS records. It also protects parts of the header, derives
Initial keys from a value anyone can see, and covers the replay risk of
0-RTT.

## Key claims

- TLS 1.3 usually needs one round trip, and zero on a repeat connection. "Absent packet loss, most new connections can be established and secured within a single round trip; on subsequent connections between the same client and server, the client can often send application data immediately, that is, using a zero round-trip setup." (1)
- TLS handshake messages ride directly on QUIC; QUIC replaces the TLS record layer. "instead of carrying TLS records over QUIC (as with TCP), TLS handshake and alert messages are carried directly over the QUIC transport, which takes over the responsibilities of the TLS record layer" (3)
- The two cooperate rather than stack. "Rather than a strict layering, these two protocols cooperate: QUIC uses the TLS handshake; TLS uses the reliability, ordered delivery, and record layer provided by QUIC." (3)
- Application data goes in QUIC frames, not TLS records. "Rather, they send it as QUIC STREAM frames or other frame types, which are then carried in QUIC packets." (3)
- Initial packet keys come from the client's Destination Connection ID. "use a secret derived from the Destination Connection ID field from the client's first Initial packet." (5.2)
- So Initial packets aren't secret. "Initial packets are not protected with a secret key, so they are subject to potential tampering by an attacker." (7)
- The packet number and some header bits are encrypted too (header protection). "Parts of QUIC packet headers, in particular the Packet Number field, are protected using a key that is derived separately from the packet protection key and IV." (5.4)
- 0-RTT can be replayed, as in TLS. "The use of 0-RTT in QUIC is similarly vulnerable to replay attack." (9.2)
- QUIC's own frames are safe to replay; the application data isn't. "QUIC is not vulnerable to replay attack, except via the application protocol information it might carry." (9.2)
