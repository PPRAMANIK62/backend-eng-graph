## Summary

Defines the ChaCha20 stream cipher, the Poly1305 authenticator, and the
ChaCha20-Poly1305 AEAD (2018, replacing RFC 7539). The introduction
explains why the internet wanted a second cipher next to AES.

## Key claims

- AES is fast and widely supported. "On most modern platforms, AES is anywhere from four to ten times as fast as the previous most-used cipher, Triple Data Encryption Standard (3DES -- [SP800-67])" (1)
- ChaCha20 is a stream cipher. "This document defines the ChaCha20 stream cipher as well as the use" (Abstract)
- If AES were found weak, there would be no fast fallback. "If future advances in cryptanalysis reveal a weakness in AES, users will be in an unenviable position." (1)
- AES is slower without hardware support. "while AES is very fast on dedicated hardware, its performance on platforms that lack such hardware is considerably lower." (1)
- Many AES software implementations leak through cache timing. "many AES implementations are vulnerable to cache-collision timing attacks ([Cache-Collisions])." (1)
- ChaCha20 is about three times as fast as AES without AES hardware. "It is considerably faster than AES in software-only implementations, making it around three times as fast on platforms that lack specialized AES hardware." (1)
- 3DES was the only other widely supported cipher, and much slower, so a standby cipher was needed. "With the only other widely supported cipher being the much slower 3DES, it is not feasible to reconfigure deployments to use 3DES." and "the need for a standby cipher" (1)
- ChaCha20 resists timing attacks. "ChaCha20 is also not sensitive to timing attacks" (1)
- Poly1305 is a fast MAC. "The Poly1305 authenticator.  This is a high-speed message authentication code." (1)
