## Summary

A 2016 update to RFC 1035 and RFC 1123 that makes TCP support mandatory
for every general-purpose DNS implementation. Older text said resolvers
SHOULD support TCP, and some people read that as optional. Bigger
responses (DNSSEC, IPv6) and spoofing defenses made TCP matter more.

## Key claims

- Most DNS traffic is UDP; TCP is used for zone transfers and for messages over the old 512-byte limit. "Most DNS [RFC1034] transactions take place over UDP [RFC768]." (1)
- DNSSEC and IPv6 made responses bigger and pushed more traffic to TCP. "The growing deployment of DNS Security (DNSSEC) and IPv6 has increased response sizes and therefore the use of TCP." (1)
- TCP also helps against spoofed-source reflection attacks. "The need for increased TCP use has also been driven by the protection it provides against address spoofing and therefore exploitation of DNS in reflection/amplification attacks." (1)
- Some implementers read the old SHOULD as "TCP is optional". "some implementors have taken the text quoted above to mean that TCP support is an optional feature of the DNS protocol." (1)
- The old text it replaces is RFC 1123 section 6.1.3.2: resolvers MUST support UDP and SHOULD support TCP. "DNS resolvers and recursive servers MUST support UDP, and SHOULD support TCP, for sending (non-zone-transfer) queries." (1, quoting RFC 1123)
- The new rule: every general-purpose implementation supports both. "All general-purpose DNS implementations MUST support both UDP and TCP transport." (5)
- That includes stub resolvers in the OS. "Stub resolver implementations (e.g., an operating system's DNS resolution library) MUST support TCP" (5)
