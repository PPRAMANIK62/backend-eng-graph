## Summary

The CoDel spec (Experimental). Before describing the algorithm it
explains where queuing delay comes from: buffers at bottleneck links,
the difference between a burst that drains (good queue) and a standing
queue that only adds delay (bad queue), and why buffers kept growing.

## Key claims

- Full buffers are an old problem made worse by cheap memory. "the problem has only gotten worse as growth in memory density per Moore's Law fueled an exponential increase in buffer pool size." (1. Introduction)
- Worst at the consumer edge. "This "bufferbloat" problem [BLOAT] has become increasingly important throughout the Internet but particularly at the consumer edge." (1. Introduction)
- Buffers exist for good reasons: to absorb bursts. "Network buffers exist to absorb the packet bursts that occur naturally in statistically multiplexed networks." (1. Introduction)
- Where queues form. "Packet queues form in buffers facing bottleneck links, i.e., where the line rate goes from high to low or where many links converge." (3. Understanding the Building Blocks of Queue Management)
- Worked example: a TCP window of 25 packets through a path whose bandwidth-delay product is 20 packets settles into a 5-packet standing queue; a 30-packet window gives 10, with no change in sending rate. "Nothing can be inferred about the sending rate from the queue size, and any queue other than transient bursts only creates delays in the network." (3)
- Good vs bad queue. "In terms of the delay experienced, the "good" queue goes away in about a round-trip time, while "bad" queue hangs around for longer, causing delays." (3)
- Sojourn time: the time a packet spends in one buffer. "the time a packet departs the buffer minus the time the packet arrived at the buffer." (2. Conventions and Terms Used in This Document)
- Settings: target 5 ms and interval 100 ms for the terrestrial internet, based on unbloated RTTs up to about 100 ms. "Using this RTT gives a minimum TARGET of 5 ms and INTERVAL of 100 ms." (4. Overview of the CoDel AQM)
- Example of a bottleneck: a fast link feeding a slow one. "(say, a high-speed Ethernet link into a limited DSL uplink)" (3. Understanding the Building Blocks of Queue Management)
- CoDel watches time in the queue, not queue length. "use of packet sojourn time as the observed datum (rather than packets, bytes, or rates)" (1. Introduction)
- Persistent delay above target triggers drops. "When the estimator finds a persistent delay above TARGET, the controller enters the drop state where a packet is dropped" (4. Overview of the CoDel AQM)
