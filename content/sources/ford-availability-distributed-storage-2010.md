## Summary

OSDI 2010 paper from Google. A year of failure data from tens of
Google storage cells (1,000 to 7,000 nodes each). Shows that node
failures come in correlated bursts, that racks are a real failure
domain, that rack-aware placement helps, and that models assuming
independent failures overestimate availability by orders of magnitude.

## Key claims

- Most large failure bursts are rack or multi-rack events. "We find that most large bursts of failures are associated with rack- or multirack level events." (Abstract/intro list, section 4)
- Correlated failures are the key thing a model must capture. "the critical element in models of availability is their ability to account for the frequency and magnitude of correlated failures." (Introduction)
- Definition of a failure domain. "By failure domain, we mean a set of machines which we expect to simultaneously suffer from a common source of failure, such as machines which share a network switch or power cable." (Section 4)
- About 37% of failures are part of a burst of two or more nodes. "we observe that 37% of failures are part of a burst of at least 2 nodes." (Section 4.1)
- Bursts come in two shapes: many at once (power outage) and slow, evenly spaced (rolling reboots or upgrades). "Such correlated failures can be seen, for example, as part of rolling reboot or upgrade activity at the datacenter management layer." (Section 4.2)
- Only 3% of large bursts had all nodes on different racks. "For larger bursts of at least 10 nodes, we find only 3% have all their nodes on unique racks." (Section 4.2, Figure 8)
- Racks, switches and power domains are frequent causes. "Domain-related issues, such those associated with physical racks, network switches and power domains, are frequent causes of correlated failure." (Section 4.3)
- Rack-aware placement: no two chunks of a stripe on the same rack. "A rack-aware policy is one that ensures that no two chunks in a stripe are placed on nodes in the same rack." (Section 5.2)
- Rack-aware placement typically triples stripe MTTF. "using a rack-aware placement policy increases the stripe MTTF by a factor of 3 typically." (Section 5.2)
- Ignoring correlation overestimates availability by at least two orders of magnitude. "failing to account for correlation of node failures typically results in overestimating availability by at least two orders of magnitude" (Section 8.3)
- More replicas help less when failures are correlated. "The gain in availability achieved by increasing the replication number, for example, grows much more slowly when we have correlated failures." (Section 8.3)
- Most unavailability came from planned reboots. "We observe that the majority of unavailability is generated by planned reboots." (Section 3)
- Planned kernel upgrades are a major source of correlated failures. "planned kernel upgrades are a major source of correlated failures." (Conclusions)
- Correlation dominates everything else. "We find that correlation among node failures dwarfs all other contributions to unavailability in our production environment." (Conclusions)
