## Summary

Defines the EdDSA signature scheme and its two standard versions,
Ed25519 and Ed448 (2017). The introduction lists why EdDSA is easier to
use safely than older signature schemes.

## Key claims

- EdDSA doesn't need a fresh random number per signature. "The use of a unique random number for each signature is not required;" (1)
- Small keys and signatures. "EdDSA uses small public keys (32 or 57 bytes) and signatures (64 or 114 bytes) for Ed25519 and Ed448, respectively;" (1)
- Security level. "Ed25519 is intended to operate at around the 128-bit security level and Ed448 at around the 224-bit security level." (1)
- EdDSA survives hash collisions (PureEdDSA). "EdDSA provides collision resilience, meaning that hash-function collisions do not break this system (only holds for PureEdDSA)." (1)
- High performance. "EdDSA provides high performance on a variety of platforms;" (1)
- A large quantum computer would break both. "A sufficiently large quantum computer would be able to break both." (1)
- More resistant to side-channel attacks. "It is more resilient to side-channel attacks;" (1)
- Ed25519 hashes with SHA-512 internally. "H(x) | SHA-512(dom2(phflag,context)||x)" (5.1, parameter table)

## My notes

- Other signature schemes (ECDSA) need a fresh secret random number per
  signature; the RFC implies it but doesn't say what goes wrong. Leave
  that out.
