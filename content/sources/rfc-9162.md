## Summary

Certificate Transparency 2.0 (2021): public, append-only logs of issued
TLS certificates. Read here for its precise definition of a Merkle tree
over a list of entries, and for the two proofs a Merkle tree allows:
that an entry is in the log, and that a newer log extends an older one.

## Key claims

- CT logs are append-only. "Certificate Transparency aims to mitigate the problem of misissued certificates by providing append-only logs of issued certificates." (1)
- The logs detect misissuance rather than prevent it. "The logs do not themselves prevent misissuance, but they ensure that interested parties (particularly those named in certificates) can detect such misissuance." (1)
- Merkle trees make append-only provable. "The append-only property of each log is achieved using Merkle Trees, which can be used to efficiently prove that any particular instance of the log is a superset of any particular previous instance" (1)
- A leaf hash. "MTH({d[0]}) = HASH(0x00 || d[0])." (2.1.1)
- An interior hash. "MTH(D_n) = HASH(0x01 || MTH(D[0:k]) || MTH(D[k:n]))" (2.1.1)
- Why the 0x00 and 0x01 prefixes. "Note that the hash calculations for leaves and nodes differ; this domain separation is required to give second preimage resistance." (2.1.1)
- The tree needn't be full. "Note that we do not require the length of the input list to be a power of two." (2.1.1)
- Inclusion proof. "A Merkle inclusion proof for a leaf in a Merkle Tree is the shortest list of additional nodes in the Merkle Tree required to compute the Merkle Tree Hash for that tree." (2.1.3)
- Consistency proof. "is the list of nodes in the Merkle Tree required to verify that the first m inputs D[0:m] are equal in both trees." (2.1.4)
- Same idea as a history tree. "This Merkle Tree is essentially the same as the history tree proposed by [CrosbyWallach], except our definition handles non-full trees differently." (2.1.1)
- A log can cheat by showing different views to different clients. "The log auditing mechanisms described in this document can be circumvented by a misbehaving log that shows different, inconsistent views of itself to different" clients (1)
- So logs are still trusted parties. "Therefore, it is necessary to treat each log as a trusted third party." (1)
