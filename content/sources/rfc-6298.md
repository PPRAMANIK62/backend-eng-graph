## Summary

The standard algorithm (2011, replaces RFC 2988) for how long a
TCP sender waits for an ACK before resending: a smoothed round-trip
time plus four times its variation, with a floor, doubling on each
timeout. RFC 9293 requires it.

## Key claims

- The retransmission timer is what guarantees delivery when no feedback comes back. "The Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) [Pos81] uses a retransmission timer to ensure data delivery in the absence of any feedback from the remote data receiver." (1)
- A TCP may be more conservative, never more aggressive. "However, a TCP MUST NOT be more aggressive than the following algorithms allow." (1)
- The sender keeps two variables, SRTT and RTTVAR. "To compute the current RTO, a TCP sender maintains two state variables, SRTT (smoothed round-trip time) and RTTVAR (round-trip time variation)." (2)
- Before any measurement, the RTO starts at 1 second (it used to be 3). "the sender SHOULD set RTO <- 1 second" (2.1)
- First sample R: SRTT = R, RTTVAR = R/2, RTO = SRTT + max(G, K*RTTVAR) with K = 4. (2.2)
- Later samples: RTTVAR = (1 - beta) * RTTVAR + beta * |SRTT - R'|, SRTT = (1 - alpha) * SRTT + alpha * R'. "The above SHOULD be computed using alpha=1/8 and beta=1/4" (2.3)
- Minimum RTO of 1 second. "Whenever RTO is computed, if it is less than 1 second, then the RTO SHOULD be rounded up to 1 second." (2.4)
- The large minimum is there to avoid spurious retransmissions, and might change. "Research suggests that a large minimum RTO is needed to keep TCP conservative and avoid spurious retransmissions [AP99]." (2.4)
- The spec leaves room for a smaller floor later. "at some future point, research may show that a smaller minimum RTO is acceptable or superior." (2.4)
- An upper bound of at least 60 s may be set. "A maximum value MAY be placed on RTO provided it is at least 60 seconds." (2.5)
- Karn's algorithm: don't take RTT samples from retransmitted segments. "RTT samples MUST NOT be made using segments that were retransmitted (and thus for which it is ambiguous whether the reply was for the first instance of the packet or a later instance)." (3)
- The timestamp option removes that ambiguity. "the timestamp option removes the ambiguity regarding which instance of the data segment triggered the acknowledgment." (3)
- Never retransmit sooner than one RTO after the last send. "a segment is never retransmitted too early, i.e., less than one RTO after the previous transmission of that segment." (5)
- On expiry, resend the earliest unacknowledged segment. "Retransmit the earliest segment that has not been acknowledged by the TCP receiver." (5.4)
- Then double the timer. "The host MUST set RTO <- RTO * 2 (\"back off the timer\")." (5.5)
- If the SYN timed out, reset the RTO to 3 s once data starts. (5.7)
- A new RTT sample after recovery can collapse the RTO back down. (5, note after 5.7)

## My notes

- Linux ignores the 1 s minimum: tcp_rto_min_us defaults to 200 ms
  (kernel-ip-sysctl). The RFC allows that research may justify a smaller
  minimum.
