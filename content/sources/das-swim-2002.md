## Summary

The SWIM paper (DSN 2002, Cornell). Group membership for large clusters
without all-to-all heartbeats: each member probes one random peer per
protocol period, asks k others to probe it indirectly if the direct ping
gets no ack, and spreads membership changes by piggybacking them on those
same ping and ack messages (infection style). A suspicion step and
incarnation numbers cut false positives; round-robin target selection
bounds detection time. Some symbols are lost in the PDF's text layer.

## Key claims

- All-to-all heartbeating doesn't scale: load grows with the square of group size. "traditional heart-beating protocols, which either impose network loads that grow quadratically with group size, or compromise response times or false positive frequency" (Abstract)
- SWIM separates failure detection from spreading membership updates. "Unlike traditional heartbeating protocols, SWIM separates the failure detection and membership update dissemination functionalities of the membership protocol." (Abstract)
- Expected detection time and per-member load don't depend on group size. "Both the expected time to first detection of each process failure, and the expected message load per member, do not vary with group size." (Abstract)
- Updates ride on pings and acks, spreading like gossip. "Information about membership changes, such as process joins, drop-outs and failures, is propagated via piggybacking on ping messages and acknowledgments." (Abstract)
- A process losing messages looks the same as a failed one. "since a process that is losing messages is indistinguishable from one that has failed" (1)
- Heartbeats to a central server create a hot spot; to everyone, quadratic load. "Sending all heartbeats to a central server leads to hot-spot creation." (1)
- Dissemination latency grows logarithmically with group size. "the dissemination latency in the group grows slowly (logarithmically) with the number of members" (1)
- Membership is weakly consistent: lists at different members can differ at a given moment. "membership lists at different members need not be consistent across the group at the same (causal) point in time." (1)
- You can't build a detector over an asynchronous network that is both strongly complete and never wrong; SWIM keeps completeness and tries to keep false positives low. "[6] proved the impossibility of building a failure detector over an asynchronous network that is both accurate (no false detections) and strongly complete." (2)
- Each period: ping one random member, and if no ack comes in time, ask k random members to ping it (ping-req). "selects members at random and sends each a ping-req( ) message." (3.1)
- Indirect probing avoids blaming the target for congestion on one path. "is to avoid the effect of any congestion on the network path between" (3.1)
- The protocol period must be at least three times the round-trip estimate. "Note that the protocol period has to be at least three times the round-trip estimate." (3.1)
- Piggybacking means no extra packets: only pings, ping-reqs and acks. "The only packets generated by SWIM then are pings, ping-reqs and acks, thus giving a constant expected message overhead per group member." (3)
- Suspicion: an unresponsive member is first marked suspected, and declared faulty only if it doesn't refute before a timeout. "This prespecified time-out thus effectively trades off an increase in failure detection time for a reduction in frequency of false failure detections." (3)
- An overloaded but alive process can miss pings and be wrongly declared failed. "It is also possible that a process is perturbed for small durations of time, e.g. on an overloaded host." (3)
- Named after the spread of gossip or epidemics. "We call this an infection-style dissemination mechanism as information spreads in a manner analogous to the spread of gossip in society, or epidemic in the general population [8]." (4.1)
- Such an epidemic spreads exponentially fast. "Such an epidemic process spreads exponentially fast in the group" (4.1)
- Infection-style spreading tolerates failures and message loss. "the infection style of dissemination is resilient to process failures and loss of messages within the network" (4.1)
- Suspicion reduces but doesn't eliminate false positives. "This mechanism reduces (but does not eliminate) the rate of failure detection false positives." (4.2)
- Incarnation numbers: only the member itself can raise its own, to refute a suspicion with a newer Alive message. "A member’s incarnation number is initialized to 0 when it joins the group" (4.2; the PDF drops the member symbol: "A member ’s incarnation number")
- Confirm beats Alive and Suspect; Alive beats Suspect only with a higher incarnation. "Alive messages override Suspect messages, and Confirm messages override both Suspect and Alive messages" (4.2)
- Round-robin probing bounds the time to detection: at most twice the group size in protocol periods. "the time interval between the occurrence of a failure and its detection at member is no more than two times the group size (in number of protocol periods)." (3; symbol dropped in the PDF)
- Prototype settings: protocol period 2 s. "the protocol period used was 2 sec." (5)
