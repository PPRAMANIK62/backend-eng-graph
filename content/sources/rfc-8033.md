## Summary

The PIE spec (Experimental), from Cisco and CableLabs authors. PIE
estimates how long packets wait in the queue and drops arriving
packets at random with a probability it adjusts to hold that delay
near a target. A variant is mandatory in DOCSIS 3.1 cable modems.

## Key claims

- Status: Experimental. (header)
- Definition of bufferbloat. "Bufferbloat is a phenomenon in which excess buffers in the network cause high latency and latency variation." (Abstract)
- Why buffers got big: operators asked for as much as possible, and memory got cheap. "To avoid packet drops, many service providers or data-center operators require vendors to put in as much buffer as possible." (1)
- Loss-based TCP fills them. "TCP continuously increases its sending rate and causes network buffers to fill up." (1)
- Drops come late, when buffers are already full. "However, drops and marks usually occur when network buffers are full or almost full." (1)
- RED needs tuning, so operators leave it off, and it controls length, not latency. "most network operators don't turn RED on." (1)
- PIE drops at random, based on queuing latency and its trend. "Congestion detection, however, is based on the queuing latency instead of the queue length (as with RED)." (1)
- Target latency 15 ms. "The target latency value, QDELAY_REF, SHOULD be set to 15 milliseconds." (4.2)
- No per-packet timestamps needed, so it's cheap in hardware. "The design does not require per-packet timestamps, so it incurs very little overhead and is simple enough to implement in both hardware and software." (Abstract)
- DOCSIS 3.1 mandated a PIE variant for cable modems in 2013. "… CableLabs' Data-Over-Cable Service Interface Specification 3.1 (DOCSIS 3.1) specification [DOCSIS_3.1] mandated that cable modems implement a specific variant of the PIE design" (1)
